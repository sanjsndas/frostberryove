About Frostberry Oven Lane

Seasonal, tested recipes and kitchen stories from a tiny oven on a quiet lane in the Pacific Northwest.

I'm Maren Ellison, a recipe developer and writer. I started this blog in 2018 as a way to document the food I was cooking in a drafty apartment kitchen — the successes, the failures, and the meals that tasted like home.

Frostberry Oven Lane is not a polished production. It's a real kitchen with real constraints: a small oven, a limited counter, and a sourdough starter that sometimes gets neglected. But it's also a place where I've learned that good food doesn't require perfection — just curiosity, a little patience, and a willingness to try again.

Every recipe here is tested at least twice in my home kitchen, and many are tested by volunteer readers before they're published. I write about what I actually cook: seasonal vegetables, simple baking projects, and dinners that come together after a long day.

— Maren

What I believe

🌾

Seasonal first

I cook with what's growing, what's at the market, and what's in the pantry. Seasons shape the recipes here — you'll find tomato confit in August and braised beans in January.

🧪

Tested, not guessed

Every recipe is tested at least twice. I note substitutions, weights, and the small details that make a difference. If something didn't work, I'll tell you.

🏡

Real kitchens only

No professional equipment, no impossible techniques. Just a home oven, a few good pans, and a belief that cooking should be approachable and satisfying.

Why "Frostberry Oven Lane"?

Behind my house, there's a narrow lane that runs along the edge of a small woodland. In late summer, wild blackberry bushes grow thick along the fence — the kind of berries that are sweet, tart, and stubbornly resilient. My neighbor calls them frostberries, because they hold on until the first frost.

When I was trying to name this blog, I kept coming back to that lane. It felt like the right metaphor for what I wanted to do here: use what's around you, cook with what's in season, and find beauty in the ordinary.

“The name is a reminder that good food doesn't have to be fancy. It just has to be made with attention.”

The "oven" part is literal. My kitchen has a small, temperamental oven that runs a little hot. I've learned to work with it, and most of the recipes here are written for ovens like mine — imperfect, real, and entirely capable of making something delicious.
